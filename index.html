Uranus:

Uranus is the seventh planet from the Sun and the third-largest planet in the Solar System. It is an ice giant made mostly of hydrogen, helium, and icy materials such as water, ammonia, and methane. Uranus appears blue-green because methane gas in its atmosphere absorbs red light and reflects blue light. It is one of the coldest planets, with temperatures reaching about -224°C (-371°F).

Uranus rotates on its axis in about 17 hours and is unique because it spins on its side, with a tilt of about 98 degrees. It takes about 84 Earth years to complete one orbit around the Sun. The planet has a faint ring system and 28 known moons, including Titania, its largest moon. Uranus also has strong winds and a strange seasonal cycle because of its extreme tilt.

Uranus is named after the ancient Greek god of the sky, making it the only planet named after a Greek rather than a Roman god. Because of its unusual sideways rotation and icy atmosphere, Uranus is one of the most interesting planets in the Solar System. Scientists continue to study Uranus to better understand ice giant planets and their role in the formation of planetary systems.
